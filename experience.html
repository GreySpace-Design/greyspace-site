EXPERIENCE

Experience measured in working hardware.

Complex hardware demands more than good designs. Our team has tackled challenges across prototype builds, systems definition, test infrastructure, electrical integration, and manufacturing processes.

01 / HARDWARE BUILD LEADERSHIP

From incomplete definition to operating hardware.

THE CHALLENGE

A high-complexity, 100+ kWh industrial energy-storage prototype required the build system, suppliers, and cross-functional decisions to catch up with the design.

THE WORK

Established build documentation, procurement and supplier coordination, integration sequences, issue resolution, and hands-on hardware execution.

THE RESULT

Supported the transition from incomplete engineering definitions to an operating prototype in under a year.

02 / SYSTEMS ARCHITECTURE

Turning an evolving product into a defined system.

THE CHALLENGE

Development was already underway on a complex mobile energy-storage product without a common set of formal system requirements.

THE WORK

Established a requirements framework and review workflow; reconstructed system expectations, decomposed subsystem boundaries, defined interfaces, and translated applicable standards into engineering constraints.

THE RESULT

A shared technical definition, traceable requirements, and a foundation for verification and certification planning.

03 / TEST INFRASTRUCTURE

Test hardware that evolves with the product.

THE CHALLENGE

Firmware and subsystem teams needed physical test capability without accumulating a separate one-off bench for each configuration.

THE WORK

Designed modular electrical and mechanical bench architecture, selected controls and connectors, fabricated harnesses, and integrated reconfigurable subsystem interfaces.

THE RESULT

Reusable electrical test infrastructure supporting firmware development and subsystem integration.

04 / ELECTRICAL INTEGRATION

Making four prototype harness sets buildable.

THE CHALLENGE

A second-generation electrical architecture needed to become real, manufacturable harness assemblies across four complex prototype builds.

THE WORK

Defined interfaces, connectors, pinouts and interconnect diagrams; partitioned harnesses, coordinated 3D routing and external fabrication, and used cross-document reviews to catch mismatches.

THE RESULT

Documented, physically integrated harness architecture supporting four prototype units and spare assemblies; inconsistencies surfaced before further integration.

05 / PROCESS IMPROVEMENT

A simple tool, a measurable difference.

THE CHALLENGE

A precision-seal installation process was slow and difficult to execute consistently.

THE WORK

Developed a purpose-built hand tool and simplified the installation operation.

THE RESULT

Installation time fell from minutes to seconds. Pressure-test failures declined from over 40% to approximately 15% over the same period; the change should not be attributed solely to the tool without further evidence.
